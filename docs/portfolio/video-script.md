# Walkthrough video — script and cue sheet

`docs/assets/portfolio/video/northstar-walkthrough.mp4` · 2:30 · 1920×1080 · produced by
`python tools/portfolio_video.py` from the workbook's own renders and live captures of Power
BI Desktop. The preview, `preview.gif`, is the live Power BI segment.

The voice-over below is written to be read at a measured pace over the cues; the video has no
audio track of its own.

| time | on screen | voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Title card | Northstar is a synthetic private-equity-backed industrial group: twelve legal entities, four currencies, three ERP systems and about four hundred million dollars of revenue. This is the platform that takes it from native ERP extracts to board reporting. |
| 0:12 | Architecture diagram | Three ERPs are harmonised to one chart of accounts, the group is consolidated in five explicit layers, governed marts carry one definition per measure, and the same numbers reach an Excel workbook and a Power BI report. Controls and lineage run beside every step. |
| 0:23 | Excel — Executive summary, full sheet then the KPI tiles | The Excel management reporting workbook opens on the executive summary: revenue, margin, the three EBITDA definitions, cash, net debt, covenant leverage and headroom, each with its governed variance and the direction that variance is favourable in. |
| 0:38 | Excel — the attention list | The attention list is generated by rule from the variance mart — the largest unfavourable variances and any covenant headroom under a turn — and each comment states the calculation, not an interpretation. |
| 0:44 | Excel — Cash flow and liquidity | Cash flow is derived from balance-sheet movements and checked every month; liquidity is shown against the minimum-cash policy. |
| 0:51 | Excel — Debt and covenants | Leverage is measured against the limit the agreement itself sets for each year, tested at the year ends, and shown as indicative in between. |
| 0:59 | Excel — Consolidation and control status | The consolidation sheet shows what each of the five layers contributed, and beside the numbers, every phase's control results — read from the registers, never typed. |
| 1:08 | Power BI — Executive Overview | The Power BI executive report is generated from declarations on a governed semantic model. The overview is built to be read in thirty seconds: four first-tier figures, six second-tier, the year so far, the full-year outlook and the unit that needs attention. |
| 1:19 | Power BI — the business unit slicer, Industrial Services chosen | Choosing a business unit narrows everything that is unit-level — revenue, EBITDA, headcount — and leaves what is Group-level, and the tiles say which is which. |
| 1:30 | Power BI — back to the Group | The Actual line stops at the August close; budget and forecast carry on. Nothing on the page is typed, computed locally or scaled twice. |
| 1:36 | Power BI — Business Units & Entities | Units sum to the Group, and each variance bar is coloured by the measure's own favourability. |
| 1:43 | Power BI — Debt & Covenants | The covenant page gives a verdict only where the agreement gives one. Between test dates it reads *Indicative*. |
| 1:52 | Power BI — EBITDA & Variance Bridge | Statutory, management-adjusted and covenant EBITDA are three definitions, kept apart and bridged. |
| 1:58 | Power BI — Consolidation & Controls | The consolidation bridge follows the same period basis as the headline it reconciles to — the year-to-date bridge sums to year-to-date statutory EBITDA to the cent — and every control count comes from a register. |
| 2:08 | Power BI — P&L, the account hierarchy expanded | The income statement drills from line to caption to account on a governed hierarchy. |
| 2:16 | Closing card | Why the numbers can be trusted: a model can balance, render and still be wrong. Six hundred and seventy-seven automated controls, eighty-three fault fixtures each caught by the control named for it, reconciliation across every artefact, deterministic builds — and a register of what the controls found and what each finding changed. |
